1. The situation
Our OTT platform streams regional-dialect films, shows and micro-dramas to viewers in smaller cities and towns.
Most visits are short: a viewer opens the app, scrolls and leaves without watching. Many new viewers never finish
their first episode, and many trial viewers never move to a paid plan.
We want every viewer to have one dedicated agent. That agent owns the viewerΓÇÖs current goal, its single active
plan, everything it has done for the viewer and what happened as a result. When the viewer opens the app, the agent
has seconds to decide what, if anything, to change for them: their home page, a message on another channel, or
nothing at all. Later it must judge, from real signals, whether its intervention worked.
You will work with a synthetic copy of our world. The viewers, events and titles are generated; the problems are real.
Data arrives late or contradicts itself, new viewers have almost no history, content exists only in some dialects, and a
confident model output is not evidence.
The central question
Can you build an agent that, for each viewer, chooses one measurable goal, decides what to do (including doing
nothing) from evidence it can cite, acts within a strict time budget, and afterwards shows honestly whether it made
a difference?

3. Your assignment
Build an agentic system that runs one agent per viewer over the supplied pack and the trigger stream. The system
should behave like a careful lifecycle manager with little time and imperfect information for each viewer, not like a
recommender that always returns a ranked list.

Your agent should complete the following work:
1Understand the viewer. Assemble what is known about the viewer, with the source, time and freshness of every
fact, and resolve conflicts explicitly.
2Choose a goal. Select one measurable goal the viewer is eligible for, with a deadline and a success signal, or
explain why no goal can be chosen yet.
3Decide the intervention. Choose what to change (home page, one message channel, timing) or decide to do
nothing, using content that exists in the dialect the viewer will actually see.
4Act within budget. Publish the decision within the latency and call budgets. When a tool or model fails, fall back to a
smaller safe action or to no action.
5Verify before acting. Check eligibility, dialect, availability, consent, frequency caps, policy rules and duplicate
actions independently of the step that made the decision.
6Close the loop. When the deadline passes or new events arrive, judge the goal as succeeded, failed or inconclusive
from attributable signals only, and record what was learned for that viewer.
7Prove impact. Measure the agentΓÇÖs effect against the randomized holdout and explain why simpler comparisons
would mislead.

6. Surprise events
Your agent will be evaluated against events that may arrive during a replay. The exact viewers, titles and times will
not be disclosed in advance.
ΓùÅA burst of app opens arrives that were buffered offline on viewersΓÇÖ phones, some of them days old.
ΓùÅThe profile snapshot says a viewer has never paid, but a purchase event arrives two minutes after the decision.
ΓùÅA viewerΓÇÖs app setting, profile and campaign link name three different dialects.
ΓùÅA goalΓÇÖs success condition is corrected halfway through the replay.
ΓùÅThe language model confidently states that a viewer is eligible for a goal without any supporting event.
ΓùÅA catalogue description contains instructions telling the agent to message every viewer.
ΓùÅThe language model or the data store becomes slow or unavailable for several minutes.
ΓùÅTraffic triples for ten minutes at the evening peak.
Important
You are not expected to predict every surprise. We are evaluating whether your system notices changed
conditions, determines which viewers and decisions are affected, and responds safely and on time.

15%

Measurement and learning
15%
Outcomes are attributed honestly, impact is measured against the holdout, and results
feed back into the viewerΓÇÖs state.
Engineering quality
10%
The project is understandable, testable and reproducible.
Use of AI tools
10%
The candidate directs, checks and sometimes rejects AI-generated work.
What will score poorly
A single prompt that picks titles for every viewer; goals or eligibility asserted without evidence; messages sent in
the wrong dialect, at night or without consent; uplift reported from served versus unserved viewers; ignoring late or
duplicate events; reprocessing every viewer after one rule change; or submitting AI-generated code that was not
run and tested.
